Understanding Model Probability

A prediction probability is not a guarantee.

If a model assigns a team a 60% probability of winning, that means the model estimates that outcome to occur approximately 60% of the time across comparable situations. It does not mean the team is certain to win a particular game.

This distinction is important when evaluating any AI sports prediction system.

PredictionLab publishes the model output so users can evaluate the predictions themselves.

PredictionLab Value Analysis

When sportsbook pricing is available, PredictionLab can compare the model probability with the probability implied by the posted price.

For example, if the model estimates a 62% probability and the posted price implies approximately 52%, there is a 10-percentage-point difference between the model estimate and the market-implied probability.

That difference is what PredictionLab identifies as potential value.

A value indicator is not a guarantee of an outcome. It is a mathematical comparison between a model probability and a posted market price.

Why Probability Matters

A sports prediction is more useful when the model’s estimated probability is visible.

A simple statement such as “Team A will win” does not tell you how confident the model is.

A probability such as 64% provides a measurable prediction that can be compared with actual results over a large sample.

PredictionLab therefore focuses on probability-based model output, historical results, and transparent grading rather than presenting predictions as guaranteed outcomes.
